# Costume Reference — The Sikh Bana (18th-c. Akali-Nihang)

Authentic costume reference for stage/screen or for grounding in-game art. The
**recommended canonical era is the 18th-century Khalsa Fauj / Akali-Nihang
tradition** (Punjab) — it is the origin of the martial Bana and, crucially, a
**continuous living tradition** you can verify against present-day Nihang Singhs
and Gatka akharas rather than reconstruct.

> **Respect guardrail:** the Kara, Kachera, Kirpan (and Kesh under the turban)
> are the Five Kakaars — articles of a living faith, not props. Engage a Sikh
> cultural consultant / Gurdwara, handle the turban and Kirpan with reverence,
> and never frame sacred articles comically or gorily. See the guardrail in
> [../CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md#cultural-authenticity--a-hard-guardrail).

## Dressing sequence (also the quick-change order)

1. **Kachera** — 2. **Kurta** (inner) — 3. **Chola** — 4. **Kamarkasa** (sash) —
5. **Gatra + Kirpan** — 6. **Kara(s)** — 7. **Keski/fifty then Dumalla**, tuck
shastars — 8. **Hajooria** draped.

## 1. Garments & layers

| Garment | Structure & fit | Fabric |
|---|---|---|
| **Kachera** (Kakaar) | Knee-length drawstring undergarment with a deep central gusset for full leg lift; tied with a woven **naala** (never elastic). | Fine white cotton, ~90–120 GSM. |
| **Kurta** (inner) | Simple long tunic under the Chola; wicks sweat, protects outer dye. | Light cotton. |
| **Chola** (robe) | Fitted chest/shoulders, flaring from the waist into **kali** godet panels for a wide sweeping *gher*; long tapered sleeves; side **tanni** cloth ties (no buttons/zips). | Heavy handspun **khaddar** cotton, ~180–250 GSM — deep-dyeing, weighty drape, matte under lights. |
| **Kamarkasa** (sash) | Long cloth wound several times at the waist; cinches the Chola, braces the back, holds tucked shastars. | Contrasting cotton (saffron/gold over blue). |
| **Hajooria** (neck cloth) | ~1.5–2 m scarf around the neck / over a shoulder; rooted in *seva*; doubles as a field wrap. | Soft white or saffron muslin. |

## 2. Turban architecture

**Dumalla (warrior — use this for a fighter):**

*Geometric definition (for art/asset accuracy):*
- **Symmetrical & cylindrical/domed** — wrapped **horizontally** around the head
  into an even, equally-wide cylinder rising to a dome. This is the key
  difference from a daily **Dastar**, which has a **slanted, asymmetrical V-front
  with a diagonal profile**. The Dumalla has no V and no diagonal — it reads the
  same from left and right.
- **Two-cloth foundation** — the name means *Du* (two) + *Malla* (cloth): an inner
  **keski/"fifty"** tied tightly over the topknot (Kesh) for structural height,
  then a much larger outer cloth (**10–25+ m**) wrapped systematically over it.
- **Conical layered progression** — the outer cloth wraps continuously from the
  forehead upward; each successive **pala (layer)** sits slightly higher and a
  touch narrower than the last, producing a stacked vertical extension.
- **Full coverage** — the lower horizontal bands are wound tight and **wide,
  fully covering the ears and the sides of the head** (front edge rises at the
  brow, dips low over the ears).
- **Function:** the rigid, dense layers act as literal **head armour** and safely
  anchor small shastars — a mini-kirpan or iron **chakkar** (throwing ring) tucked
  into the wraps.

*Ornaments & fabric:*
- **Chand Tora** — a metallic crescent-and-sword emblem secured **front-centre**.
- **Farla** — a loose length of cloth left to stream from the crown (rank marker).
- Fabric: light **mal-mal (muslin)** — only a light cloth wraps to 15+ m without
  crushing the wearer. Dyed deep Khalsa blue or white.

> This geometry is what `Artist.drawDumalla` renders (symmetrical dome, stacked
> palas, ear coverage, front Chand Tora, tucked chakkar) in both the game and the
> [dossier](../akaal-dossier.html).

**Dastar (standard dress turban):** 5–7 m × ~1.1 m, folded into a **pooni**, in
**4–7 visible larh**. Fabrics: **Full Voile** (soft everyday), **F74** (crisp,
sharp larh — a modern cloth; avoid for a strict 18th-c. look), **Rubia** (inner),
**mal-mal** (dumalla).

## 3. Accessories & metalwork — Sarbloh

All-iron/steel, **no gold or precious metal** (deliberate austerity); blacked /
naturally patinated, never bright chrome.

| Item | Placement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Kara** (Kakaar) | Wrist (traditionally right); Nihangs often wear several up the forearm | Plain sarbloh ring — eternity + restraint. |
| **Kirpan** (Kakaar) | Left hip on the **Gatra** | Curved single-edge, worn edge-forward. |
| **Gatra** | Woven baldric, right shoulder → left hip, over the Chola | Blue/saffron weave, small sarbloh ring at the join. |
| **Shastars in the Dumalla** | Tucked into the dome | Chakram ringing the dome; small kirpans, khanda, bagh-nakha, tir at the front — the turban as armoury. |
| **Kamarkasa tuck** | Waist sash | Talwar, katar, peshkabz slid into the wound sash. |

**Weight note (production):** real sarbloh is heavy — cast lightweight blacked
doubles for full-day choreography, reserve genuine steel for close-ups.

## 4. Color psychology

Present as resonance, not rigid code (meanings are layered and sometimes
contested).

- **Royal / Khalsa Blue (Neela)** — color of the Akali-Nihang; the infinite sky
  and vastness of *Akaal*; the warrior's steadfast detachment. **Anchor color.**
- **Saffron / Kesri / Basanti** — sacrifice, valor, *Chardi Kala*; the color of
  the Nishan Sahib and the martyr's resolve. **The lifting accent.**
- **White (Safed)** — purity, humility, the saintly (*sant*) nature; the
  Sant-Sipahi balance. **The ground.**
- **Black (Kala)** — remembrance, resistance, mastery over ego; historically
  mourning injustice. **Reserve for a single pivotal beat.**

**Production palette direction:** anchor in deep Khalsa blue, accent with
saffron, ground with white, reserve black — a costume arc the audience feels
without being told. Matches the project [ART-DIRECTION.md](ART-DIRECTION.md).

## Authenticity tripwires (instantly betray a later era)

Visible buttons/zips/velcro · synthetic sheen · chrome or gold metal · a flat
pressed dress-Dastar on a warrior · F74/modern crisp cloth · colonial-era
(19th-c.) tailoring cues. Hide any unavoidable modern fastening under the
kamarkasa or a tanni flap.

**Primary sources:** Nihang Singhs & Gatka akharas in Punjab (Anandpur Sahib,
Amritsar) and late-1700s Sikh/Pahari miniature paintings — anchor here rather
than on film references.
